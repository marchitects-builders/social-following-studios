# Avatar Studio Immigration Law Six Sample Scripts

Status: production script package for review.

These scripts implement Rashida's six requested immigration law angles for fifteen to thirty second native TikTok and Reels style samples. They are written as reusable templates for an attorney digital twin.

Important claim rule: any client result, filing timeline, approval result, case outcome, or policy statement must be verified by the attorney before publishing. Script One contains a verified result variable instead of inventing a client result.

## Script One: Case Win

Angle: case win

Runtime target: twenty two to twenty seven seconds

On screen hook:

GREEN CARD APPROVED IN [VERIFIED TIMEFRAME]

Spoken script:

"One of our clients received a green card in [VERIFIED TIMEFRAME]. The useful part is what happened before the approval. We checked the filing category, organized the evidence before submission, answered every required question, and made sure the record told one consistent story. A fast result can never be promised, but preparation can prevent avoidable delays. If you are getting ready to file, build the record before you hit submit."

Shots and cuts:

Zero to three seconds: attorney digital twin close to camera. Large result text appears.

Three to eight seconds: cut to a clean case timeline graphic with all names and identifiers removed.

Eight to fifteen seconds: quick document closeups showing a checklist, evidence tabs, and filing sequence.

Fifteen to twenty two seconds: attorney returns to camera while PREPARE BEFORE YOU FILE appears.

Suggested B roll and graphics:

Redacted approval notice, generic timeline, organized evidence folder, filing checklist.

CTA:

"Save this before you prepare your green card filing."

Publishing gate:

Replace [VERIFIED TIMEFRAME] only with a documented client result the attorney has permission to discuss.

## Script Two: Bad Online Advice

Angle: myth bust and bad online advice

Runtime target: twenty to twenty five seconds

On screen hook:

VIRAL IMMIGRATION ADVICE CAN HURT YOUR CASE

Spoken script:

"Immigration advice that worked for one person online may be completely wrong for your case. Your history, status, entry, family relationship, prior filings, and immigration record can change the answer. Before you copy a TikTok checklist or submit a form because somebody in the comments told you to, verify that the advice actually applies to you. A thirty second video cannot see your whole record."

Shots and cuts:

Zero to three seconds: fast scroll through blurred social posts.

Three to eight seconds: attorney points toward three floating comment bubbles with conflicting advice.

Eight to sixteen seconds: cut to a simple graphic showing HISTORY, STATUS, ENTRY, PRIOR FILINGS.

Sixteen to twenty three seconds: tight attorney shot.

Suggested B roll and graphics:

Blurred social feed, conflicting comment bubbles, generic immigration forms, four factor graphic.

CTA:

"Save this, and verify advice before you file."

## Script Three: USCIS Policy and Evidence Update

Angle: policy update and denial prevention

Runtime target: twenty three to twenty eight seconds

On screen hook:

DO NOT ASSUME USCIS WILL ASK TWICE

Spoken script:

"Do not build an immigration filing around the assumption that USCIS will always give you another chance to fix missing evidence. Agency procedures and case handling can change, and some filings can be decided on the record that was submitted. Before you file, check the current form instructions, required evidence, filing edition, fee, and official USCIS guidance for your category. Your safest move is a complete record on day one."

Shots and cuts:

Zero to three seconds: official looking generic document lands on desk. Text says CHECK BEFORE YOU FILE.

Three to nine seconds: quick cuts through FORM EDITION, FEE, EVIDENCE, INSTRUCTIONS.

Nine to eighteen seconds: attorney digital twin beside a complete versus incomplete file visual.

Eighteen to twenty six seconds: official USCIS dot gov text graphic, without copying protected page design.

Suggested B roll and graphics:

Generic form cover, checklist animation, complete and incomplete folders, USCIS dot gov reminder card.

CTA:

"Save this and check the current official instructions before submitting."

Publishing gate:

Attorney must confirm the current policy language and form requirements on the publication date.

## Script Four: Immigration Helper Scam

Angle: scam warning

Runtime target: twenty to twenty five seconds

On screen hook:

IMMIGRATION SCAM WARNING

Spoken script:

"That immigration helper in your DMs could cost you your case. Guaranteed approval, cash only payments, pressure to send documents fast, and no written agreement are serious warning signs. Before you share personal information or money, verify who the person is and what they are legally authorized to do. Your immigration record is too important to hand to an unverified stranger."

Shots and cuts:

Zero to three seconds: blurred direct message appears with GUARANTEED APPROVAL.

Three to nine seconds: four warning cards appear one at a time.

Nine to seventeen seconds: attorney at desk. VERIFY FIRST fills the screen.

Seventeen to twenty three seconds: official firm website and public contact page graphic replace the fake message.

Suggested B roll and graphics:

Blurred WhatsApp or DM style message, warning icons, generic written agreement, verified firm contact graphic.

CTA:

"Send this to someone looking for immigration help."

## Script Five: Marriage Green Card Evidence Mistake

Angle: marriage green card mistake

Runtime target: twenty two to twenty seven seconds

On screen hook:

REAL MARRIAGE. WEAK FILE.

Spoken script:

"A real marriage can still have a weak immigration file. Officers look at the evidence showing how you actually share your life, not only a wedding photo. Think housing, finances, insurance, travel, correspondence, family history, and other records that genuinely fit your relationship. Do not manufacture evidence. Build an honest record that makes the relationship easy to understand."

Shots and cuts:

Zero to three seconds: two folders appear, one organized and one nearly empty.

Three to nine seconds: quick cuts through generic lease, shared account record, insurance page, photos.

Nine to seventeen seconds: attorney explains while four evidence category labels appear.

Seventeen to twenty five seconds: organized file closes.

Suggested B roll and graphics:

Generic lease, redacted joint document, photographs, calendar, organized folder tabs.

CTA:

"Save this before preparing a marriage based filing."

## Script Six: DACA Clarification

Angle: special category explainer

Runtime target: twenty two to twenty eight seconds

On screen hook:

DACA IS NOT A GREEN CARD

Spoken script:

"DACA does not automatically turn into a green card. It is a different immigration protection, and whether someone has another path depends on their individual history and the law that applies to them. Family relationships, employment options, travel history, entry history, prior immigration events, and other facts may matter. Do not assume another person's path is yours. Get your own record reviewed."

Shots and cuts:

Zero to three seconds: DACA card graphic on one side, green card graphic on the other, separated by a large NOT EQUAL sign.

Three to nine seconds: attorney digital twin explains the distinction.

Nine to eighteen seconds: six fact cards appear quickly: FAMILY, WORK, ENTRY, TRAVEL, HISTORY, PRIOR CASES.

Eighteen to twenty six seconds: attorney returns to camera.

Suggested B roll and graphics:

Generic DACA document silhouette, green card silhouette, pathway diagram, fact cards.

CTA:

"Save this and share it with someone who thinks DACA automatically becomes permanent residency."

Publishing gate:

Attorney must confirm current DACA policy and any relevant litigation or agency changes immediately before publication.

## Production Rules Across All Six

Use one credible attorney digital twin throughout the set.

Keep the opening hook under ten spoken words whenever possible.

Use hard or purposeful cuts every two to four seconds.

Add accurate captions throughout.

Use documents, timelines, messages, headlines, and simple graphics as supporting visuals.

Never invent a client, result, approval time, testimonial, or guaranteed outcome.

Blur or replace all personal information.

Use official current government sources for policy claims before publishing.

Keep each CTA native to short form content: save, share, comment, or book a consultation.

Do not publish any sample as a real attorney statement until the attorney approves the final script and factual claims.
